Human Modeling and Cognitive Alignment in Interactive Agents

Bridging Human Understanding, AI Metacognition, and Interactive Decision Making

How should AI understand humans and itself, and decide when to answer, ask, explain, defer, or act?

Proposed Workshop One-day Workshop Expected Attendance: 200-400 Workshop Date: December 11, 12, or 13, 2026, TBC Location: Atlanta / Sydney / Paris, TBC

Website URL: https://cogalign-workshop.github.io/

Recent advances in foundation models have enabled AI systems that can reason, plan, retrieve information, use tools, generate content, and participate in increasingly complex workflows. As AI systems evolve from passive predictors into interactive agents, they are increasingly judged not only by output quality, but by the quality of their interactions with humans. This workshop focuses on Human Modeling and Cognitive Alignment as an emerging interdisciplinary area connecting human modeling, AI metacognition, cognitive alignment, and interaction-policy selection.

Workshop Overview

This proposed NeurIPS 2026 workshop connects machine learning, natural language processing, robotics, human-computer interaction, cognitive science, recommender systems, AI safety, education, healthcare, affective computing, and AI companions.

The workshop centers on a shared problem across these communities: interactive AI systems must decide not only what output to produce, but what interaction policy is appropriate for a specific human, task, context, and uncertainty profile.

Central Question

How should AI systems use uncertain models of humans and themselves to select interaction policies?

Position

Selecting appropriate interaction policies under uncertainty is the next frontier of agentic AI.

Human Modeling + AI Metacognition

Human modeling and AI metacognition are not end goals themselves. They are enabling capabilities for interaction-policy selection: deciding when to answer, ask, explain, retrieve, recommend, teach, collaborate, defer, escalate, negotiate, or act.

Why Now

  • Foundation models are becoming increasingly agentic.
  • AI systems are moving into high-stakes and long-term interaction settings.
  • Human-centered AI research is active but fragmented.
  • AI companions, tutors, healthcare assistants, robots, recommender systems, and research assistants expose similar interaction-policy problems.
  • There is a need for a shared interdisciplinary research agenda.

Topics

We invite submissions related, but not limited, to the following topics:

  • Human modeling, user modeling, theory of mind, personalization, preference learning, human simulation, and social reasoning
  • AI metacognition, uncertainty estimation, calibration, self-evaluation, capability assessment, selective prediction, and risk-aware reasoning
  • Cognitive alignment, interaction policies, clarification strategies, human-aware planning, human-AI collaboration, and shared mental models
  • Foundation models, multimodal systems, agentic AI, tool-using agents, memory systems, embodied agents, and robotics
  • AI companions, affective computing, trust, emotional modeling, long-term adaptation, and human-AI relationships
  • Evaluation methodologies, benchmarks, longitudinal studies, trust calibration, and human-centered metrics
  • Applications in education, healthcare, public health, scientific discovery, journalism, recommendation systems, accessibility, finance, and social robotics

Representative Domains

Education and tutoring

Interaction policies determine when systems should teach, quiz, hint, explain, adapt, or hand control back to learners.

Healthcare and public health

Assistants must balance personalization, calibrated uncertainty, escalation, privacy, and human autonomy in high-stakes settings.

Human-robot collaboration

Robots need models of human intent, capability, workload, and trust to decide when to act, ask, wait, or defer.

AI companions and long-term relationships

Longitudinal systems raise questions about adaptation, emotional dependence, transparency, and responsible support.

Scientific discovery

Research assistants must reason about a scientist's goals, evidence needs, uncertainty, and when to suggest or challenge hypotheses.

Journalism and information gathering

Interactive agents must decide when to retrieve, verify, ask clarifying questions, expose uncertainty, or refuse unsupported claims.

Recommendation systems

Recommenders need to model preference uncertainty, context, long-term effects, and when explanation or exploration is appropriate.

Accessibility and public services

Public-facing agents must adapt to diverse needs while preserving fairness, dignity, transparency, and reliable escalation paths.

Ethics and Responsible Research

Human modeling and cognitive alignment raise important questions about privacy, manipulation, autonomy, fairness, emotional dependence, and societal impact. These issues are especially relevant for AI companions, educational systems, healthcare assistants, recommendation systems, and social robots.

The workshop welcomes work on responsible human modeling, trustworthy personalization, calibrated reliance, transparency, governance, and human autonomy.

Expected Outcomes

  • Establish Human Modeling and Cognitive Alignment as a coherent interdisciplinary research area
  • Connect fragmented research communities
  • Identify common benchmarks, datasets, and evaluation methodologies
  • Clarify open scientific challenges and future directions
  • Foster collaborations across ML, NLP, robotics, HCI, cognitive science, recommender systems, AI safety, and domain applications
  • Develop shared vocabulary for human modeling, AI metacognition, and interaction-level alignment
  • Host accepted papers, slides, posters, and post-workshop resources on the website

How This Workshop Differs from Related Workshops

This workshop is complementary to existing workshops but focuses on a different unit of analysis: how uncertain models of humans and uncertain models of AI capability should jointly determine interaction policies.
