Human Modeling and Cognitive Alignment in Interactive Agents

Human modeling, AI metacognition, and interaction-policy selection

How should AI understand humans and itself, and decide when to answer, ask, explain, defer, or act?

Proposed NeurIPS 2026 Workshop One-day workshop December 11-13, 2026 100-200 expected attendees
Scope Speakers Organizers CFP Dates Schedule

Workshop Scope

Foundation-model agents increasingly reason over long horizons, use tools, retrieve evidence, generate code and content, and participate in complex human workflows. As they move from passive predictors to interactive agents, success depends not only on what they can do, but on how they decide what to do next with people.

Central question: How should AI systems use uncertain models of humans and themselves to select interaction policies?

Human Modeling

Inferring goals, preferences, knowledge, trust, uncertainty, cognitive load, intent, and emotional needs.

AI Metacognition

Estimating capability boundaries, evidence strength, risk, reliability, and when the system should not act alone.

Interaction Policies

Choosing when to answer, ask, explain, retrieve, recommend, teach, collaborate, escalate, defer, or act.

  • Human state modeling, including theory of mind, knowledge and uncertainty modeling, trust, cognitive load, goals, and intent
  • AI metacognition, including capability boundary assessment, selective prediction, abstention, reliability estimation, and out-of-distribution self-assessment
  • Interaction policy learning, including clarification, human-aware planning, escalation, and deferral
  • Personalization and longitudinal adaptation
  • Evaluation and benchmarks, including human-centered metrics and trust calibration
  • Applications in education, healthcare, robotics, AI companions, scientific discovery, and recommendation

Submission Format

Main text up to 9 pages, excluding references and appendices. Submissions are double-blind and non-archival.

Review Focus

Relevance, scientific quality, novelty, impact, clarity, and evaluation or reproducibility rigor where appropriate.
